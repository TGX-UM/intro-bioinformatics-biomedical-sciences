# Biological databases

*Course: BBS2002, bachelor Biomedical Sciences, year 2. Total time: about 7 hours, in three
practicals.*

Biomedical research runs on databases. This module teaches you to get answers out of them by
following real genes through the main resources: IGF-1 and growth, the cancer gene APC, and a
gene of your choice linked to cancer, ageing or Alzheimer disease. Everything runs in a
browser. There is nothing to install and no code to write.

## What you will learn

After this module you can:

- find which diseases a gene is linked to, and which variants cause them, in OMIM (Practical 1;
  [OMIM](guide/omim.md));
- read a pathway diagram in WikiPathways and place a gene, protein or metabolite in its
  biological context (Practical 1; [WikiPathways](guide/wikipathways.md));
- look up a small molecule and its biological roles in ChEBI and PubChem (Practical 1;
  [ChEBI](guide/chebi.md), [PubChem](guide/pubchem.md));
- describe a gene's location, transcripts, RefSeq records, expression and phenotypes in Ensembl
  and NCBI Gene (Practicals 1 and 2; [Ensembl](guide/ensembl.md),
  [NCBI Gene](guide/ncbi-gene.md));
- describe a variant: its alleles, consequences, frequencies and clinical classification, in
  dbSNP, ClinVar and Ensembl (Practical 2; [dbSNP and ClinVar](guide/dbsnp-clinvar.md));
- describe a protein's function, location, modifications, interactions and structure in UniProt
  (Practical 2; [UniProt](guide/uniprot.md));
- follow one gene through all these databases and explain why they do not always agree
  (Practical 3; [Why use databases at all?](guide/why-databases.md),
  [Identifier cheat-sheet](guide/identifiers.md)).

## Before you start

You need a web browser and nothing else. The [Reference guide](guide/index.md) describes every
database used here: what it holds, how to read its pages on the 2026 interfaces, and the traps
to avoid. Keep it open while you work. Each practical opens with a short introductory lecture
and a few videos or readings.

## About the answers

Answers sit under their questions, in a collapsed **Solution** block. Try the question first,
then open the solution to check your answer.

These are live databases that are re-released several times a year. Every answer therefore
carries the date it was checked and the release it was checked against, and answers whose
numbers are likely to move are marked **release-volatile**. If what you see disagrees with what
is written here, the page is probably out of date: please
[open an issue](https://github.com/TGX-UM/intro-bioinformatics-biomedical-sciences/issues).
