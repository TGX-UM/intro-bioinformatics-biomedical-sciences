# Biological databases

*Course: BBS2002, bachelor Biomedical Sciences, year 2. Total time: about 8 hours, in two
practicals (2A and 2B) of about 2 hours preparation and a 2-hour computer session each.*

For most Biomedical Sciences students these two practicals are the first real bioinformatics
they do. You learn to get answers out of the databases that biomedical research runs on, by
looking up genes you have already met in the tutorial cases: IGF-1 and growth in practical 2A,
the cancer gene APC in practical 2B. Everything runs in a browser. There is nothing to install
and no code to write.

## What you will learn

After these practicals you can:

- find which diseases a gene is linked to, and which variants cause them, in OMIM (2A
  preparation; [OMIM](guide/omim.md));
- read a pathway diagram in WikiPathways and place a gene, protein or metabolite in its
  biological context (2A; [WikiPathways](guide/wikipathways.md));
- look up a small molecule and its biological roles in ChEBI and PubChem (2A session;
  [ChEBI](guide/chebi.md), [PubChem](guide/pubchem.md));
- describe a gene's location, transcripts, RefSeq records, expression and phenotypes in Ensembl
  and NCBI Gene (2A and 2B; [Ensembl](guide/ensembl.md), [NCBI Gene](guide/ncbi-gene.md));
- describe a variant: its alleles, consequences, frequencies and clinical classification, in
  dbSNP, ClinVar and Ensembl (2B; [dbSNP and ClinVar](guide/dbsnp-clinvar.md));
- describe a protein's function, location, modifications, interactions and structure in UniProt
  (2B preparation; [UniProt](guide/uniprot.md));
- follow one gene through all these databases and explain why they do not always agree (2B
  session; [Why use databases at all?](guide/why-databases.md),
  [Identifier cheat-sheet](guide/identifiers.md)).

## Before you start

You need a web browser and nothing else. The [Reference guide](guide/index.md) describes every
database used here: what it holds, how to read its pages on the 2026 interfaces, and the traps
to avoid. Keep it open while you work.

The preparations are the same for everyone. The two computer sessions come in **three
versions** (A, B and C), each built around a different gene from the BBS2002 tutorial cases.
Choose one version and work through it. If you have time left, or later to practise, do one of
the other versions as well. Each session ends by comparing your results with someone who did a
different version.

## For BBS2002 students

Bring your prepared answers to the computer session, on a laptop or on paper: you need them
during the session. Each session opens with a short quiz on the preparation, followed by a
demonstration of the databases. If you miss a session, the extra-practice assignment of that
practical is your catch-up assignment. Schedules, the quiz and sign-off are on Canvas.

## About the answers

Answers sit under their questions, in a collapsed **Solution** block. Try the question first,
then open the solution to check your answer.

These are live databases that are re-released several times a year. Every answer therefore
carries the date it was checked and the release it was checked against, and answers whose
numbers are likely to move are marked **release-volatile**. If what you see disagrees with what
is written here, the page is probably out of date: please
[open an issue](https://github.com/TGX-UM/intro-bioinformatics-biomedical-sciences/issues).
