# Not covered in this guide

The practicals use a deliberately small set of resources. Some others you may need later:

- **microRNAs**: RNAcentral, <https://rnacentral.org/>, gathers non-coding RNA sequences including miRNAs from miRBase and other sources; miRDB, <https://mirdb.org/>, predicts miRNA targets.
- **Epigenetics and gene regulation**: ENCODE, <https://www.encodeproject.org/>, for DNA methylation, histone marks and chromatin accessibility data; the Regulation tracks in the Ensembl Genome browser show regulatory features.
- **Protein structure**: the Protein Data Bank, <https://www.rcsb.org/> or <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/pdbe/>, for experimental structures; AlphaFold DB, <https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/>, for predicted structures of nearly every protein.
- **Protein families and domains**: InterPro, <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/>.
- **Pathways, expert-curated**: Reactome, <https://reactome.org/>, a peer-reviewed pathway knowledgebase with detailed reaction-level diagrams.
- **Gene expression across many experiments**: Expression Atlas, <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gxa/home>; for protein-level tissue and cell expression, the Human Protein Atlas, <https://www.proteinatlas.org/>.
- **Gene Ontology annotations in full**: QuickGO, <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/>.
- **Population variation in depth**: gnomAD, <https://gnomad.broadinstitute.org/>, the largest public collection of human variant frequencies.
- **Drug targets and disease associations**: Open Targets Platform, <https://platform.opentargets.org/>, which combines genetic, expression, literature and drug evidence for target-disease links.
